## D. Physical revalidation — two workstreams, and what they do and do not block

**D.1 Transfer quadrature.** Five conditions, none met: a signed per-channel
response comparison inside 5e-4; full-domain coverage or a bounded remainder for
the 0.6092 M² uncovered at order 2; a stated accuracy for the reference;
remainder radii from outside the certified pair (one route, not the only one);
a funded independent validation budget fixed before launch.

**D.2 Order-resolution attribution.** An appropriate physical control and a new
result. D.1 does not supply it.

**Scope of the block.** These two are prerequisites for a strong
continuum-acquisition claim or a physical order-resolution claim. This candidate
makes neither: section 1.3 disclaims both, section 8 marks the transfer
quadrature not qualified, and the index-sum arm is labelled a compression
control throughout. They are therefore not launch prerequisites for *this*
finite-model paper. They become prerequisites the moment a stronger claim is
added. Neither is being described as both deferred and unavoidable.

## E. Author decisions — required before any submission

1. **Scope.** Submit a finite-model information and recovery paper that states
   these gaps plainly, or run D.1 and D.2 first and make the stronger claim.
   Calling the work finite-model does not make an unresolved physical claim
   publishable, and a ready token here is not permission to submit.
2. **The `eta` limitation.** Whether to publish with section 9 item 4 as
   written, or to authorize the recomputation that would close it.
3. **Bibliography depth.** How much full-text verification to require before
   submission, given that no claim in the paper currently rests on more than the
   verified level.
4. **Venue and formatting**, which fixes length, figure format and reference
   style.
